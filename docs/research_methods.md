# Research methods: expected vs actual reaction (overreaction labels)

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: partial. Items marked [GK] are general knowledge, NOT verified in this pass; items marked [V] were seen in a fetched or searched source.

## 0. Verdict on Laya
- [V] Laya (Convai Innovations, 2026-09-18, Apache 2.0): 421M ModernBERT-large classification head (512 ctx), plus 322M multilingual. Built for routing, invoices, moderation, guardrails. Zero-shot accuracy 0.362 (random 0.318), ECE 0.466 uncalibrated. The model card says it is "a fast base to specialise, not a zero-shot decision engine". No finance-return evidence. https://aiweekly.co/node/13777 , https://innfactory.ai/en/ai-models/laya/
- Conclusion: not a ready answer. At best it is one more ModernBERT-large encoder to fine-tune, which you can do directly with plain ModernBERT. Skip it.

## 1. Labeling (abnormal returns)
- [GK] Standard: market model or Fama-French/sector-ETF benchmark, estimated on a pre-event window (e.g. -250..-30 days, min ~100 obs), CAR over [0,+1], [0,+2], [0,+5]. For small caps use a robust benchmark: size-matched or sector-matched return, plus beta from a short window. Winsorize (1/99).
- [GK] After-hours and pre-market filings: use the EDGAR acceptance timestamp (not the filing date). Convert to the first tradable open: filed after 16:00 ET or on a weekend means day 0 is the next trading day's open; filed before 09:30 means day 0 is the same day. Measure from the prior close (or from the open if intraday data is available).
- [GK] Overlapping events: multiple 8-Ks for the same firm within the window (e.g. 2.02 and 7.01 on the same day, or amendments). Collapse by (ticker, trading day), concatenate the texts and union the item codes. Drop or flag events whose windows overlap an earnings date unless that is the event of interest.
- [GK] Survivorship: include delisted, merged and bankrupt names (CRSP-like data; free options are limited, so state the bias openly). Universe of 600 current small caps = survivorship bias; sampling a point-in-time universe is better. Use the as-of market cap rather than today's.
- [V] Related labeling precedent: an 8-K Item 4.01 text classifier predicting the sign of CAR (J. Financial Data Science, doi 10.1016/j.jfds.2018.08.001) https://digitalcommons.bryant.edu/finjou/75 ; 8-K taxonomy of 119 event types, 292,984 filings 2022-2026, event-study validation (arXiv 2607.08346) https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08346
- Design implication: "expected reaction" = model prediction of CAR from text + item code + pre-event features, trained out-of-fold. Overreaction = actual CAR[0,1] minus the OOF prediction. Better and cheaper: predict the drift CAR[2,20] from (actual reaction, expected reaction, text) and test whether the residual mean-reverts.

## 2. Text representation (8 GB VRAM, free)
- [V] FinMTEB (EMNLP 2025): 64 finance datasets, 7 tasks, 15 models. Domain-tuned models beat general ones, general MTEB scores correlate weakly with finance performance, and bag-of-words beats dense embeddings on finance STS. Fin-E5 is a 7B model (too big for 8 GB without quantization). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10990 , code github.com/yixuantt/FinMTEB
- [V] Chen-Kelly-Xiu: LLM news embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa, OPT, LLaMA) beat bag-of-words/SESTM for return prediction, especially with negation. FinBERT was not confirmed as a baseline in the paper. https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11540
- [V] Choi et al. "From Text to Alpha" (arXiv 2510.03195): "LLM as extractor, embedding as ruler", reporting more than 2x risk-adjusted alpha vs an NER baseline (8-K scope not confirmed). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03195
- [V] Chronologically consistent LLMs (look-ahead bias in pretrained models): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206 . Leakage trap: an LLM whose training cutoff is after the event may "know" the outcome. Prefer small encoders trained/fine-tuned by you, or check the pretraining cutoff against the earliest events.
- [GK, not benchmarked here] Practical stack: frozen embeddings (bge-small/base, gte-base, e5-base, nomic-embed-text via Ollama) of the press-release body (Exhibit 99.1), chunked to the 512-token limit and mean-pooled, then PCA to 32-64 dims, into LightGBM. Plain TF-IDF + ridge as the baseline (FinMTEB shows BoW is hard to beat on some tasks). Fine-tuning ModernBERT-base or FinBERT with a regression head on CAR is feasible on 8 GB (bf16/fp16, batch 8-16, 512 tokens) but with 30-50k noisy labels the gain over frozen embeddings + GBM is unproven; test it last. On the RX 7600 under Windows, check ROCm/DirectML support before planning on PyTorch GPU training (not verified).
- Why qwen3:1.7b failed: asking a small LLM for bull/bear is label-noise matching, not a reaction model; the market reaction depends on the surprise relative to expectations, which the text alone does not give. Use the LLM only to extract structured fields (guidance raised/lowered, amount of dilution, offering size, going-concern, customer loss) and feed those to the GBM.

## 3. Model on top, validation, skill
- [V] Purging/embargo and CPCV (Lopez de Prado, AFML ch. 7): purge training samples whose label windows overlap the test labels, embargo after test blocks, CPCV gives a distribution of OOS paths and PBO. Implementations: skfolio CombinatorialPurgedCV https://skfolio.org/generated/skfolio.model_selection.CombinatorialPurgedCV.html ; mizarlabs https://mizarlabs.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mizarlabs.model.html ; overview https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation
- [GK] Walk-forward by calendar time (expanding window, refit yearly), purge = label horizon (20 trading days if the drift label is used), embargo about 5 days. Group by ticker too: the same company's text style repeats, so also run a ticker-grouped holdout to test whether you learn the company rather than the signal.
- [GK] Leakage traps: (1) filing timestamp vs price time; (2) features computed with data after the event (short interest is published with a lag, use the settlement-date + publication-date offset); (3) embeddings/PCA/scalers fit on the full sample (fit only on the training fold); (4) pretrained LLM knowledge of outcomes; (5) current-constituent universe; (6) duplicated boilerplate text across 8-Ks of the same company; (7) hyperparameter search on the same folds you report.
- [GK] Metrics: per-day (cross-sectional) Spearman rank IC and its t-stat via day-clustered or Newey-West errors, IC by year and by size tercile, decile spread net of costs (small caps: use at least 50-100 bps round trip as a stress), calibration plot (predicted vs realized CAR in bins), compare vs a baseline using only pre-event features and item codes. Use PBO / deflated Sharpe if many variants are tried.

## 4. Long-horizon text signals
- [V] Lazy Prices (Cohen, Malloy, Nguyen, JF 2020): 10-K/10-Q changes, 1995-2014, up to 188 bps/month long-short alpha, no announcement effect, strongest for changes in CEO/CFO language, litigation and risk factors. https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084
- NOT verified: any post-2020 out-of-sample replication or small-cap result. Only a QuantConnect student replication on the 100 most liquid stocks was found (secondary source): https://www.quantconnect.com/research/20966/filing-language-stability-as-a-selection-signal/ . [GK] Anomalies in general decay after publication and concentrate in illiquid names (McLean-Pontiff), which cuts both ways for small caps: stronger signal, higher costs.
- [V] Tone: Loughran-McDonald word-list tone has weak, inconsistent return evidence after the filing window; a thesis comparing 1994-2008 with 2008-2018 found the frequency-based method insufficient (low-quality source). Chouliaras (MPRA 65585) reports that changes in pessimism interacted with prior returns matter more than levels. https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/65585/1/MPRA_paper_65585.pdf . Risk-factor tone predicts index-level returns (FRL 2025): https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v81y2025ics1544612325007317.html
- Earnings-call language: not verified in this pass (no source found).
- Cheap implementation [GK]: cosine similarity (TF-IDF or embeddings) between the same section of consecutive 10-K/10-Q filings (Item 1A, Item 3, MD&A), plus the diff of sentence counts; use as features for the drift label (20-60 trading days) and test on small caps by size tercile and by pre/post-2020 split.
